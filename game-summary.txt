BEYOND EARTH - GAME AND MISSION SYSTEM SUMMARY
================================================

This document describes the game as it is currently implemented in this
project. It is a summary of the code, not a feature proposal. The main entry
point is index.html; the mission builder, mission manager, catalogues, flight
experience, cockpit, profile, and audio features are implemented in the js
folder.


1. WHAT KIND OF GAME THIS IS
----------------------------

Beyond Earth is a space-exploration mission planning game with a spacecraft
cockpit, astronaut profile, editable mission blueprints, engineering checks,
mission deployment, a generated flight plan, and a mission-linked science
minigame.

There are not a set of fixed, authored story missions with dialogue trees.
The player creates a mission by combining a destination, science objectives,
spacecraft architecture and equipment, journey choices, and resource limits.
The game then generates operational activity cards/phases from some of those
objectives. The choices mainly affect the saved mission design, review
warnings, generated activities, and whether Spectrum Detective is available.

Important distinction: most generated activities are planning records, not
separate playable levels. Spectrum Detective is the one registered science
minigame in the shipped code.


2. STARTUP AND MAIN SCENES
--------------------------

The opening begins with an animated launch scene. The player can skip it and
go directly to the cockpit. Otherwise, a wake-up scene asks the player to
press the primary POWER ON button; the flight-deck screens and indicators
activate before the cockpit appears. Reduced-motion settings shorten or
remove some of these waits.

The main cockpit has these stations:

  - SETTINGS: opens audio settings and the Game Data & Progress section,
    which includes a two-step factory reset with typed confirmation.
  - CONTINUE: opens Mission Control, where an existing draft or flight is
    displayed if one exists.
  - NEW MISSION: opens Mission Control for a new mission blueprint.
  - RUN MISSION: enabled only when the active mission has been approved.
  - ASTRONAUT: opens the astronaut profile.
  - ARCHIVE: currently displays cockpit feedback only; there is no connected
    archive screen or populated archive catalogue in the shipped interface.

There is one active mission at a time. Mission progress and mission history
are stored locally in browser storage, so a saved draft or flight can be
restored on the same device/browser.


3. MISSION CREATION: THE 12 PLANNING STAGES
-------------------------------------------

The mission console presents these stages in order. The progress bar also
allows direct navigation between stages after a draft has been created.
Most choices save immediately as flight notes.

01 - Mission Headquarters
  Enter a mission designation (up to 80 characters). A new draft is created
  as mission type "science". The initial resource values are 1,000 credits,
  1,000 Wh power reserve, 500 kg propellant, 100 MB data storage, and 100%
  equipment condition. The astronaut's selected colour and emblem appear in
  the planning experience.

02 - Choose a destination
  Select one of 14 catalogue targets. The page shows the destination type,
  description, scientific value, risk, travel estimate, and environmental
  readings. Selecting a destination copies its environmental conditions into
  the mission and sets the duration to the catalogue travel estimate unless
  the player has manually changed the duration.

  Destinations and catalogue travel estimates:
    Earth Orbit (0 days), Moon (3), Mars (210), Venus (120), Mercury (150),
    Jupiter (1,800), Europa (1,900), Ganymede (1,800), Saturn (2,600),
    Titan (2,600), Enceladus (2,600), Asteroids (400),
    Near-Earth Object (120), and Sun (30).

  Each destination also has a type, approximate AU distance, scientific-value
  rating, risk value, and environmental conditions. Risk is used in the
  generated plan. A risk greater than 0.6 creates a high-risk plan warning.
  Destination risks range from Earth Orbit at 0.1 to the Sun at 0.9.

03 - Choose your science
  Choose one primary objective and any number of supporting objectives.
  Selecting an already-primary objective as primary again does not clear it;
  promoting a secondary objective to primary removes it from the secondary
  list. Supporting objectives can be checked or unchecked independently.
  The page initially emphasizes destination recommendations; "EXPLORE ALL
  OBJECTIVES" reveals the full catalogue. Recommendations are guidance, not
  a lock on what can be selected.

  The 13 objective options and their coded equipment/communications needs are:

    Surface Exploration
      Required instrument: Cameras. Requires relay-capable communications.
    Geological Investigation
      Required instruments: Spectrometer and Laser Altimeter.
      Minimum data rate: 10 Mbps.
    Atmospheric Analysis
      Required instruments: Atmospheric Analyser and Infrared Spectrometer.
      Minimum data rate: 8 Mbps.
    Water or Ice Detection
      Required instruments: Radar Sounder and Thermal Sensor.
      Minimum data rate: 6 Mbps.
    Mineral Composition
      Required instruments: Spectrometer and Microscopic Imager.
      Minimum data rate: 8 Mbps.
    Magnetic Field Investigation
      Required instruments: Magnetometer and Plasma Analyser.
      Minimum data rate: 10 Mbps.
    Radiation Measurement
      Required instruments: Radiation Detector and Thermal Sensor.
      Minimum data rate: 6 Mbps.
    Seismic Monitoring
      Required instruments: Seismometer and Laser Altimeter.
      Minimum data rate: 8 Mbps.
    Sample Collection
      Required instruments: Robotic Arm and Drill.
      Minimum data rate: 8 Mbps.
    Planetary Imaging
      Required instruments: Cameras and Infrared Spectrometer.
      Minimum data rate: 7 Mbps.
    Astrobiology Environmental Investigation
      Required instruments: Atmospheric Analyser and Microscopic Imager.
      Minimum data rate: 9 Mbps.
    Orbital Observation
      Required instruments: Cameras and Infrared Spectrometer.
      Minimum data rate: 10 Mbps.
    Space Weather Monitoring
      Required instruments: Radiation Detector and Magnetometer.
      Minimum data rate: 8 Mbps.

  Preferred architectures are recommendations and appear in the catalogue.
  The design checker enforces the explicit instrument and communications
  needs, but does not reject a platform just because it is not listed as a
  preferred architecture.

04 - Choose a spacecraft
  Select one of 11 architectures. The cards describe mission class, payload
  limit, and objective coverage:
    Orbiter - 1,100 kg; orbital.
    Rover - 520 kg; surface.
    Lander - 780 kg; surface.
    Flyby Probe - 360 kg; trajectory.
    Atmospheric Probe - 480 kg; atmospheric.
    Sample Return - 920 kg; sample return.
    Space Telescope - 1,500 kg; observatory.
    Communications Satellite - 520 kg; relay.
    CubeSat - 140 kg; small satellite.
    Relay Spacecraft - 700 kg; relay.
    Autonomous Research Probe - 350 kg; autonomous.
  Changing the architecture keeps the already-selected equipment; the
  engineering review then reports incompatible components or limits.

05 - Propulsion and trajectory systems
  Choose one propulsion catalogue item or explicitly select "NO COMPONENT
  SELECTED":
    Chemical Propulsion, Electric Propulsion, Ion Propulsion, Solar Sail,
    Hybrid Propulsion, or Gravity-Assist Trajectory.
  Compatibility and a recommended option are shown, but non-recommended
  choices remain selectable and are checked in the review.

06 - Power system
  Choose one power source or no component:
    Solar Panels, Batteries, Radioisotope Power, or Hybrid Power.
  The system compares the installed equipment's power draw with the selected
  system's output.

07 - Science instruments
  Select any number of instruments with checkboxes. Required instruments for
  the selected objectives are marked REQUIRED; other items are marked
  recommended, optional, or specialised. Specialised instruments can still
  be selected, but the design review can flag incompatibilities.
  The catalogue includes Cameras, Infrared Spectrometer, Radar Sounder,
  Magnetometer, Radiation Detector, Thermal Sensor, Atmospheric Analyser,
  Seismometer, Laser Altimeter, Spectrometer, Robotic Arm, Drill, Microscopic
  Imager, Dust Analyser, and Plasma Analyser.

08 - Communications
  Choose one link or no component:
    High-Gain Antenna, Low-Gain Antenna, Relay Communications, or
    Direct-to-Earth Communication.
  The page computes the greatest minimum data rate required by the chosen
  objectives and whether any objective requires relay capability. Links
  below those needs are selectable but cause hard design issues.

09 - Navigation and thermal systems
  Choose one navigation system and one thermal-control system, or leave
  either category unselected:
    Navigation: Star Tracker, Inertial Navigation, Deep-Space Navigation.
    Thermal: Passive Radiator, Active Thermal Loop, Cryogenic Control.

10 - Journey profile
  Choose a launch configuration:
    Standard Launch, Heavy-Lift Launch, or Rideshare Launch.
  Choose a trajectory profile based on the architecture's mission class:
    Surface: Direct Transfer, Orbital Insertion, Surface Deployment,
      Gravity Assist.
    Orbital: Direct Transfer, Orbital Insertion, Gravity Assist, Flyby.
    Atmospheric/autonomous: Direct Transfer, Atmospheric Entry,
      Orbital Insertion, Gravity Assist.
  These are saved planning selections. The dossier explicitly notes that a
  launch configuration does not currently have a launch simulation attached.
  The selected profile does not independently execute a physics simulation.

11 - Mission constraints
  Set budget (100-10,000 credits, in 100-credit steps), power reserve
  (100-10,000 Wh, steps of 100), propellant (0-1,000 kg, steps of 10),
  data-storage capacity (16-2,048 MB, steps of 16), and duration
  (0-3,000 days, steps of 10). These values are saved on the mission.
  The page visualizes payload use against the architecture limit and the
  installed spacecraft power margin. Most resource sliders do not themselves
  simulate costs or automatically change mission outcomes.

12 - Mission dossier / review
  Review the destination, science objectives, spacecraft, journey, resources,
  selected equipment, generated activities, and engineering issues. The
  blueprint updates while planning and includes a small pixel-art preview.
  The dossier has four pages: COVER, SCIENCE, SPACECRAFT, and FLIGHT PLAN.
  The flight-plan page includes destination conditions, resource reserves,
  accepted trade-offs (if present), journey settings, and the engineering
  review.

  The review checks that catalogue entries exist; equipment matches the
  architecture; selected components do not conflict when conflict metadata is
  present; architecture component slots are checked when equipment has slot
  metadata; payload mass is within the architecture limit; power demand does
  not exceed power output; every objective's required instruments are
  installed; and the communications system satisfies data-rate/relay
  requirements. Destination operating limits are checked if equipment
  definitions provide them. The current built-in components do not define
  slot, conflict, or operating-limit metadata, so those checks are available
  in the framework but do not currently constrain the built-in choices.

  Review statuses are:
    SUITABLE - no warnings or hard issues; eligible for approval.
    CONDITIONAL - warnings but no hard issues; not approvable by the current
      approval operation.
    INCOMPATIBLE - at least one hard issue; not approvable.
  Approval also requires a selected destination and at least one objective.
  The player reviews the dossier and presses APPROVE MISSION to approve.


4. WHAT DESIGN CHOICES DO AND DO NOT CHANGE
-------------------------------------------

Destination and objective selections feed the generated activity plan and
design checks. Architecture and equipment affect whether the design passes
compatibility, payload, and power checks. Instrument and communications
choices determine whether objective requirements are met, and they determine
whether the science minigame is available.

Most architecture/platform ratings and component compatibility lists provide
recommendations and review feedback; they do not model full spacecraft
physics. The engineering checks use catalogue fields such as mass, draw,
output, data rate, relay capability, component compatibility, and objective
requirements. Journey options and launch configuration are stored as choices
but do not alter a simulated trajectory.

The generated plan contains success-condition descriptions ("Required
mission phases complete", "All primary objectives validated", and "Critical
resource thresholds maintained"), but the current UI does not enforce those
conditions before mission completion. Advancing a phase records the current
activity; it does not require the player to pass an activity minigame. The
player can complete an active or paused mission without completing every
phase or objective.


5. GENERATED ACTIVITIES / MISSION PHASES
----------------------------------------

The mission generator adds activity templates for supported objectives.
Activities from selected objectives are combined, duplicate activity IDs are
removed, and required activities are ordered before optional ones. The flight
panel can advance the saved current phase through this ordered list. Categories,
difficulty, and conditions are planning metadata; they are not independent
playable games in this build.

Surface Exploration
  REQUIRED - Terrain Navigation (mobility, difficulty 2):
    vehicle steering; hazard mapping.
  REQUIRED - Surface Survey (science, difficulty 3):
    camera calibration; terrain annotation.
  OPTIONAL - Waypoint Operations (operations, difficulty 2):
    route refinement; power management.

Geological Investigation
  REQUIRED - Rock Analysis (science, difficulty 3):
    sample handling; spectral scan.
  REQUIRED - Geology Mapping (science, difficulty 3):
    layer identification; surface marking.
  OPTIONAL - Sample Storage (operations, difficulty 2):
    seal check; inventory control.

Atmospheric Analysis
  REQUIRED - Atmospheric Sampling (science, difficulty 4):
    sensor calibration; gas detection.
  REQUIRED - Weather Model Update (analysis, difficulty 3):
    wind profile; temperature inversion.
  OPTIONAL - Communications Window (operations, difficulty 2):
    data transfer; relay handoff.

Water or Ice Detection
  REQUIRED - Ice Signature Scan (science, difficulty 3):
    radar readout; thermal contrast.
  REQUIRED - Plume or Subsurface Analysis (science, difficulty 4):
    volatiles detection; crust mapping.
  OPTIONAL - Thermal Pass (operations, difficulty 2):
    thermal drift correction; sensor rebalancing.

Sample Collection
  REQUIRED - Sample Acquisition (science, difficulty 4):
    contact stability; collection tool validation.
  REQUIRED - Sample Archive (operations, difficulty 3):
    container seal; sample ID logging.
  Return Trajectory (navigation, difficulty 5; launch window; re-entry plan):
    required for Sample Return architecture, optional for other architectures.

Orbital Observation
  REQUIRED - Orbit Alignment (navigation, difficulty 3):
    precision vectoring; pass timing.
  REQUIRED - Image Planning (science, difficulty 3):
    target framing; coverage selection.
  OPTIONAL - Data Downlink (communications, difficulty 2):
    signal lock; buffering.

Radiation Measurement
  REQUIRED - Sensor Calibration (science, difficulty 3):
    radiation baseline; detector check.
  REQUIRED - Shielding Check (operations, difficulty 2):
    dose model; power balancing.
  OPTIONAL - Forecast Update (analysis, difficulty 2):
    flux estimate; event alert.

Space Weather Monitoring
  REQUIRED - Solar Watch (science, difficulty 4):
    solar flare tracking; magnetometer sweep.
  REQUIRED - Flux Model Review (analysis, difficulty 3):
    storm index; system response.
  OPTIONAL - Warning Window (communications, difficulty 2):
    telemetry routing; relay handoff.

Magnetic Field Investigation
  REQUIRED - Magnetometer Sweep (science, difficulty 3):
    field calibration; vector sampling.
  REQUIRED - Plasma Trace Capture (science, difficulty 3):
    particle count; magnetic anomaly tracking.
  OPTIONAL - Field Model Update (analysis, difficulty 2):
    combined survey review; local orbital correction.

The other catalogue objectives (Mineral Composition, Seismic Monitoring,
Planetary Imaging, and Astrobiology Environmental Investigation) currently
have no activity templates in the generator. Missions using only those
objectives can therefore have zero generated phases. The plan's overall
difficulty is calculated from objective count, destination risk, and whether
an architecture is selected, then clamped to 1-10. Destination risk also
labels the plan low/moderate/high. Risks above 0.6 add a contingency-planning
warning.


6. SPECTRUM DETECTIVE SCIENCE MINIGAME
--------------------------------------

Spectrum Detective is the only minigame registered in the current game. It
can be played during an active mission only when:
  - At least one selected primary/secondary objective is one of Atmospheric
    Analysis, Water or Ice Detection, Geological Investigation, Mineral
    Composition, Astrobiology Environmental Investigation, Planetary Imaging,
    Orbital Observation, or Space Weather Monitoring; AND
  - The spacecraft includes at least one of Spectrometer, Infrared
    Spectrometer, Atmospheric Analyser, or Thermal Sensor.

The minigame creates a spectral trace and reference curves for up to five
materials: Water Ice, Carbon Dioxide, Iron-Rich Minerals, Silicate Minerals,
Methane, and Iron Oxide. The objective-to-default-material mapping is:
  Atmospheric Analysis -> Carbon Dioxide
  Water or Ice Detection -> Water Ice
  Geological Investigation -> Iron-Rich Minerals
  Mineral Composition -> Silicate Minerals
  Astrobiology Environmental Investigation -> Methane
  Planetary Imaging -> Iron Oxide
  Orbital Observation -> Silicate Minerals
  Space Weather Monitoring -> Carbon Dioxide

The target is selected from the mission objective(s) and mission ID. The
screen plots an unknown trace, presents a reference library, and has a
Wavelength Focus slider. Selecting a reference overlays its curve; Clear
Selection removes the overlay; Submit Analysis compares the selected material
ID to the target. In the current implementation, the analysis summary names
the target sample before submission, so the intended identification answer is
visible in the displayed text.

Submitting the correct reference records success, a score of 90, and a 0.9
performance rating. A wrong reference records failure, a score of 16.25, and
a 0.35 rating. The result includes a scientific-data record identifying the
target, selected reference, instrument quality, and confidence. Correct
identification marks the mission's primary objective complete, if present.
The result consumes 8 MB of data storage and reduces equipment condition by
2 percentage points. No budget or propellant is consumed by this minigame.

The game can pause/resume or end the activity. Results are attached to the
active mission; resource changes, completed objective IDs, and scientific data
are validated and stored. A minigame cannot start unless its mission is
active, and only one minigame can run at a time.


7. FLIGHT LIFECYCLE AND MISSION RECORDS
---------------------------------------

Mission status transitions in the manager:
  DRAFT -> APPROVED or CANCELLED
  APPROVED -> ACTIVE or CANCELLED
  ACTIVE -> PAUSED, COMPLETED, FAILED, or CANCELLED
  PAUSED -> ACTIVE, COMPLETED, FAILED, or CANCELLED
  COMPLETED, FAILED, and CANCELLED are terminal statuses.

The normal UI flow is:
  1. Save a draft as choices are made.
  2. Review and approve a suitable mission dossier.
  3. Use RUN MISSION / START DEPLOYMENT.
  4. View an animated deployment sequence (or reduced-motion version).
  5. The flight becomes active and the first generated activity is recorded.
  6. If Spectrum Detective is eligible, it starts automatically after
     deployment; otherwise the flight panel explains why none is available.
  7. Pause/resume, advance the current phase, play/end the science activity,
     complete the mission, or cancel it from the flight panel.

The deployment includes a pixel-art destination/spacecraft scene and profile
portrait; it is a presentation sequence, not a launch physics simulation.
Mission completion averages any recorded minigame scores into a final score.
It awards 10 experience points if at least one scored minigame result exists,
otherwise 0. Completion archives the mission, updates completed-mission IDs,
and runs any registered achievement evaluators.

The code provides achievement and research-progress infrastructure, but no
achievements or knowledge entries are registered by the shipped game scripts.
The player's stored rank defaults to RECRUIT and is not advanced by current
mission completion code. The Knowledge API can store/unlock registered
entries, but there is currently no populated in-game archive view connected to
the ARCHIVE cockpit station.

Mission history shows completed and closed flights, including cancelled or
failed records if those statuses are set. A draft can be discarded/cancelled
and is archived. The user-facing flight panel does not currently expose a
FAIL action, although the mission manager supports a failed terminal status.


8. RESOURCE AND DESIGN SYSTEMS
------------------------------

Mission resource fields include budget credits, mass, power reserve, fuel,
data storage, communications margin, and equipment condition. Resource
changes are recorded with reason, source, before/after values, and timestamp.
Negative balances are rejected for bounded resources; equipment condition is
kept between 0 and 1. The mission manager initializes resource values and
records resource changes and consumed amounts.

Spacecraft design facts calculate selected equipment mass, power draw, and
power output from the component catalogue. Hard review issues include
incompatible architecture, unknown selected component, exceeded payload,
exceeded power output, missing objective instrument, unsupported data rate or
band, and missing relay capability when required. Missing design information
can instead produce a warning.

The selected equipment catalogue contains:
  Propulsion: Chemical, Electric, Ion, Solar Sail, Hybrid, Gravity-Assist.
  Power: Solar Panels, Batteries, Radioisotope Power, Hybrid Power.
  Communications: High-Gain Antenna, Low-Gain Antenna, Relay Communications,
    Direct-to-Earth Communication.
  Instruments: Cameras, Infrared Spectrometer, Radar Sounder, Magnetometer,
    Radiation Detector, Thermal Sensor, Atmospheric Analyser, Seismometer,
    Laser Altimeter, Spectrometer, Robotic Arm, Drill, Microscopic Imager,
    Dust Analyser, Plasma Analyser.
  Thermal: Passive Radiator, Active Thermal Loop, Cryogenic Control.
  Navigation: Star Tracker, Inertial Navigation, Deep-Space Navigation.


9. COCKPIT SANDBOX, PROFILE, AUDIO, AND PRESENTATION
----------------------------------------------------

The cockpit contains interactive controls for cabin lighting, thermal
comfort, power bus, communications, life support, thermal system, simulated
power distribution, a systems monitor, a maintenance panel, and a flight
note/window discovery. The systems monitor has POWER, THERMAL, COMMS, and
LIFE SUPPORT pages. Values update as switches/dials are used; the screen
explicitly labels these readings as simulated and says they do not affect
mission resources or mission state. Maintenance self-checks are local-only.
The window note reads: "The blue planet's clouds look like a tiny rabbit
when the cabin lights are low." These interactions are cockpit toys, not
mission-system controls.

The astronaut profile saves a full name, callsign, generated BE- identifier,
colour family, emblem/sticker, and expression. It offers seven colour
families (purple, pink, yellow, blue, green, red, black/grey), seven emblems
(butterfly, rose, star, crescent moon, ringed planet, shooting star,
winged heart), and seven expressions (happy, calm, sleepy, surprised,
excited, wink, shy). The colour palette changes the ship/game interface
theme; the profile identity is shown in planning and deployment.

Audio settings include master mute, master volume, music, sound-effects, and
ambience levels. Preferences are saved on the device. Sound is implemented
with Web Audio where available, including interface feedback and launch,
panel, mission-completion, and achievement cues.

The Settings dialog's factory reset removes all game-owned browser storage,
including campaign/profile records and audio preferences, then reloads the
game into its first-time launch sequence. It preserves unrelated site data.

The interface uses generated canvas/pixel-art scenes for the launch, cockpit,
planet previews, mission blueprint, and deployment. It responds to a
reduced-motion preference and includes keyboard-accessible controls and
status announcements in the mission/profile/cockpit interfaces.


10. DATA AND IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
---------------------------------

The destination, objective, architecture, and spacecraft component
definitions used by the current mission UI are registered directly by
js/mission-builder.js. The similarly named JSON files in data are not used by
the current page scripts. Mission definitions and history, progression,
knowledge unlock IDs, companion preferences, and astronaut profile are saved
under the `beyond-earth-data:` localStorage namespace; audio preferences use
the standalone `beyond-earth-audio-v1` key. No sessionStorage, IndexedDB,
cookies, or Cache API persistence is used by the current page scripts.

The mission manager enforces one active mission, schema validation, status
transitions, approval requirements, mission history, and minigame result
validation. The test suite includes coverage for approval, minigame lifecycle,
resource changes, progression, persistence, scoped minigame input, and
isolated cockpit interactions.
